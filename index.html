That Cheap Deal Can Cost You $80,000

A bargain parcel, a $20,000 house, a town that pays you to move in — the catch is never in the headline. It's in the deed, the tax rolls, and the county office nobody calls. Here's the kind of thing I keep finding:

Land catch #1

Landlocked — no legal access

The photos show a road. The deed doesn't. Without a recorded easement, you may own land you can't legally drive to.

Land catch #7

The failed perc test

No perc, no septic. No septic, no house. Some counties keep failed test results on file — if you know to ask for them.

Land catch #12

Back taxes ride along

In many counties, unpaid property taxes stay attached to the land — meaning the "bargain" comes with someone else's bill.

Land catch #19

"Owner financing" traps

Miss one payment on some land contracts and you can lose the land and everything you've paid. It's in the fine print.

House catch #8

The insurance-bill trap

The $20,000 house is real — until the insurer sees the old roof, the wiring, and the flood map. On the coast the real premium can run $500–$1,200 a month while a lender only quotes $150, enough to sink the loan on a "cheap" house.

Relocation catch #3

"No income tax" isn't "no taxes"

Some no-income-tax states quietly make it back on property tax and fees. Texas and New Hampshire have no income tax — and some of the highest property taxes in the country. A middle-income Texan can pay more total tax than a Californian. Weigh income, property, and sales tax together.
